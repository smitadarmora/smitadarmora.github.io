About

At ACER I support researchers on UIC's Lakeshore HPC cluster and work on how people learn to use these systems well. I also work with the HEPiX Benchmarking Working Group on HEPScore4GPU, a GPU benchmark built from real high-energy physics applications.

I came to research computing from physics. My PhD and postdoctoral work were on the ATLAS experiment: searches for supersymmetry using multivariate and machine-learning methods, calibration of the Tile Calorimeter, and operating distributed computing on the Worldwide LHC Computing Grid. I've held positions at Argonne National Laboratory, Fermilab and UT Arlington, and I've taught undergraduate physics.

Current work

GPU benchmarking for high-energy physics

Measuring how real HEP applications (event generation, full detector simulation, AI-based simulation) scale across GPUs and how much power they draw, as input to HEPScore4GPU and to the application developers.

HPC system literacy and user support

Studying what researchers need to move from submitting jobs to understanding the systems they run on, and how support teams can scale that help.
